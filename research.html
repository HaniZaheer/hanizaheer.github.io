Research

My work runs in two directions: investigating interesting astrophysical signals and optimizing quantum sensors to search for them.

Quantum Sensing

Searching for axion dark matter with magnetic resonance force microscopy

Axion dark matter coupled to electrons acts like a weak oscillating magnetic field on electron spins. We show how magnetic resonance force microscopy, which reads out spin signals as tiny forces on a mechanical resonator, can search for this coupling, and we project its sensitivity.

E. Kashi, M.H. Zaheer, R. Petery, and S. Singh

Phys. Rev. D (accepted, 2026) · arXiv:2512.12120

Quantum metrology algorithms for dark matter searches with clocks

Ultralight scalar dark matter would make fundamental constants oscillate, shifting quantum clock frequencies. We propose a broadband dynamical decoupling protocol and show that it can extend the sensitivity bandwidth to higher frequencies.

M.H. Zaheer, N.J. Matjelo, D.B. Hume, M.S. Safronova, and D.R. Leibrandt

Phys. Rev. A 111, 012601 (2025)

Dark Matter Phenomenology

Multimessenger astronomy beyond the Standard Model: New window from quantum sensors

Violent astrophysical events could emit bursts of ultralight bosons alongside electromagnetic waves, neutrinos and gravitational waves. We show how networks of quantum sensors could detect these bursts as a new messenger and tie them to conventional observations.

J. Arakawa, M.H. Zaheer, V. Takhistov, M.S. Safronova, J. Eby, and C. Cheung

J. Cosmol. Astropart. Phys. 2026 (02), 026 (2026)

Detection of bosenovae with quantum sensors on Earth and in space

Dense clumps of self-interacting ultralight dark matter can collapse and explode in a “bosenova”, releasing a burst of relativistic waves. We investigate detection prospects of such events in the vicinity of the Earth using terrestrial and space-based sensors. Covered by Live Science (2023).

J. Arakawa, J. Eby, M.S. Safronova, V. Takhistov, and M.H. Zaheer

Phys. Rev. D 110, 075007 (2024)

Bosenovae with quadratically-coupled scalars in quantum sensing experiments

We extend the bosenova analysis to scalars that couple quadratically to Standard Model fields, which changes the signal in important ways, for example through screening of the scalar field by the Earth, and map out where quantum sensing experiments could detect it.

J. Arakawa, M.H. Zaheer, J. Eby, V. Takhistov, and M. Safronova

J. High Energy Phys. 2024, 222 (2024)
